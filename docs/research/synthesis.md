# Design and ethical persuasion

Working interpretations · 2026-11-09 · Not validated findings about shoppers.

## Brand archetype hypotheses

UNIQLO's everyday comfort language suggests an Everyperson reading; technical framing offers an alternative functional interpretation. Buck Mason's heritage references suggest Explorer or Creator, though this may simply explain construction. ASKET's detailed specification framing suggests Sage, while proportion options may support an inclusive Everyperson reading. These labels are researcher hypotheses, not brand declarations.

## Cialdini review

| Principle | Evidence / question | Ethical direction for later work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reciprocity | No gift or exchange mechanism established | Offer useful care/fit guidance without compulsory sign-up |
| Commitment and consistency | No sequence assessed | Optional saved preferences with clear consent and easy reversal |
| Social proof | Buck Mason B1 uses popularity language; ASKET A2 displays reviews | Use authentic, dated reviews; disclose incentives and moderation; never invent counts |
| Authority | Technical product information in U1/A2 may signal expertise | Attribute specifications and substantiate performance claims |
| Liking | Heritage storytelling in B1 could create affinity; effect untested | Use honest stories and inclusive imagery, avoid fabricated personas |
| Scarcity | No verified scarcity cue recorded | Show only real stock limits; no resetting countdowns |
| Unity | Heritage may suggest shared identity; no explicit unity mechanism confirmed | Invite participation without exclusion or pressure |

For the conceptual framework, consult the [author's overview of persuasion principles](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/). The table is an ethical audit and a set of future design possibilities, not a claim that all competitors use all principles.

## Modernism and postmodernism

The text-based evidence supports tentative functional clarity readings: product naming, fit information, and specifications could align with modernist priorities. Buck Mason's historical military/sportswear references can be read as cultural quotation, a possible postmodernist cue, but heritage language alone does not establish a postmodern visual design. Screenshots are required before making claims about grids, typography, minimalism, eclecticism, or irony.

## Questions for the future T-shirt site

Could a clear fit guide reduce uncertainty? Would evidence-backed fabric explanations help shoppers understand price? Could an honest brand story create relevance? Test these with participants before choosing an identity. Research may guide a later brand/storefront phase; this repository does not implement that phase.
